Corporate Innovation through Technology Convergence and Crossing the Chasm: The Case of Bodyfriend

Korea's massage chair market grew rapidly from KRW 20 billion in 2007 to KRW 1 trillion in 2020, propelled by COVID-19. In a market dominated by Japanese firms until the mid-2000s, the latecomer Bodyfriend reached first place globally in 2017, ten years after its founding in 2007. This case study analyses the technology strategy through which Bodyfriend differentiated its products and the management strategy with which it crossed the chasm in the massage chair market.

Research summary
  • With interest in rest and health rising since COVID-19, the massage chair market has been growing rapidly.
  • Korea's massage chair market was dominated by Japanese firms until the mid-2000s, but the Korean latecomer Bodyfriend entered the industry and took the market lead within a short period.
  • This study examines the technology strategy through which Bodyfriend differentiated its products.
  • It also examines the management strategy it used to cross the chasm in the massage chair market, and draws out the significance and implications for management of technology.

1. Introduction

The modern massage chair combining electronic and mechanical functions was first made by Japan's Fuji Medical Instruments in 1954, and until the early 2000s Korea's market was closer to a luxury goods market dominated by expensive Japanese products. The picture changed from the mid-2000s as Korean firms — Bodyfriend, Hutech Industry, Bokjung Jehyung and others — entered. Bodyfriend in particular overtook the Japanese firms in 2017 to reach first place globally (per Frost & Sullivan: Bodyfriend 8.1%, Panasonic 7.7%, Inada Family 7.2%). It is worth noting that as a device that once merely delivered massage through vibration evolved into a sophisticated healthcare device offering body composition analysis and relief for musculoskeletal conditions, Bodyfriend led that change through technology convergence.

2. The research subject

A massage chair comprises four core elements: massage balls, frame, air cells and reclining. Because many components are combined, the process is hard to automate and most work is done by hand. The product is heavy and bulky and comes at a high price: Bodyfriend's "The Pharaoh S" weighs 145 kg and costs about KRW 6.8 million, more than three times the price of a side-by-side refrigerator of similar size and weight (about KRW 1.8 million).

The massage chair market has several distinctive features. It has spread mainly in East Asia, where massage culture is developed, with penetration of 23% in Japan and 10–15% in Korea against under 1% in the United States. And because the chairs are large and heavy, few households buy more than one, so advertising competition is fierce: advertising and promotion spending as a share of revenue in 2022 was 6.9% at Bodyfriend, 10.7% at Hutech Industry and 6.5% at Bokjung Jehyung — far above Samsung Electronics' 2.0% over the same period. This means massage chair companies find it hard to put funds into R&D and facility investment. Founded by an entrepreneur trained as a computer scientist, Bodyfriend grew through excellent R&D-based functionality and a strategy easing the price burden, becoming a mid-sized company with revenue of KRW 522 billion, operating profit of KRW 24.1 billion and 1,485 employees at the end of 2022.

3. Theoretical background

The study uses three theories. Technology convergence, the concept Kodama (1991) applied to mechatronics as a fusion of mechanical and electronic technology, means the chemical combination of technologies of different character. The chasm is the demand trough Moore (1991) identified between the early market (innovators and early adopters) and the mainstream market (early and late majority); pragmatic early-majority buyers expect a product already proven and free of problems. The rental business is a service that lends goods for a fee over a set period without transferring ownership, providing the experience of use; in Korea it was popularised in the late 1990s when Woongjin Coway applied it to water purifiers.

4. The case

4.1 Technology analysis (patents)

Bodyfriend's patent applications (excluding lapsed, abandoned and rejected filings) total 435 for 2012–2022, or 394 when limited to massage chairs. Filings that had stayed in single digits from 2012 to 2017 rose sharply from 2018, peaking at 112 in 2021. The variety of IPC codes assigned (at subclass level) also grew markedly, from one to six types in 2015–2017 to nine to 23 types in 2018–2022.

The 394 patents carry 34 IPC codes in all; the five most frequent are A61H (52.5%, massage and physiotherapy), A61B (11.8%, diagnosis and surgery), A47C (7.7%, chairs), G16H (5.6%, healthcare informatics) and A61N (2.9%, electrotherapy, magnetotherapy and ultrasound therapy). Notably, 24 IPC codes appeared for the first time in 2018–2022, and those patents concern AI, IT, biotechnology and robotics — evidence that healthcare functions are expanding beyond the massage chair's original purpose. Patents carrying two or more different IPC codes also rose, to 68 in 2021, developing into a form in which various technologies converge and are applied to one another.

By comparison, Korean competitors Bokjung Jehyung and Hutech Industry filed only around 25 patents each with just eight IPC codes. Japan's Inada Family (143 filings, 24 codes) and Fuji Medical Instruments (215 filings, 9 codes) had a filing record, but A61H — the massage function code — accounted for 73.0% and 84.3% of their filings respectively. This indicates that the Japanese firms concentrated their R&D on the massage chair's original massage function.

4.2 R&D investment and organisation

Bodyfriend's ordinary R&D spending was under KRW 500 million until 2014, first passed KRW 10 billion in 2018 and rose to KRW 24.9 billion in 2022. Its share of revenue also rose from 2.90% in 2018 to 4.77% in 2022, presumably reflecting a greater need to secure technological capability after winning a patent suit against Inada Family in 2017. Against competitors (0.5% at Bokjung Jehyung and 1.1% at Hutech Industry in 2022) this is the highest level in the industry.

Organisationally, the company established a convergence R&D centre in 2011, a design R&D centre in 2013 and a medical R&D centre in 2016. About 200 researchers — roughly 13% of all employees — work across the three centres, and the medical R&D centre in particular recruited orthopaedic, psychiatric and neurosurgical specialists along with neuroengineers to converge AI, IT, BT and robotics. As a result the company implemented healthcare functions such as body composition analysis and treatment for disc conditions, developing products differentiated from competitors'.

4.4 Management strategy — crossing the chasm

In a Korea Consumer Agency survey (December 2021), consumers ranked performance (41.7%), rental fee (13.6%) and design (10.0%) as the most important factors in choosing a massage chair. What they wanted, in other words, was a massage chair with excellent performance and good design at a low financial burden through rental. The market of the early 2000s, dominated by expensive Japanese products, met none of these criteria, and was stuck in a chasm of high prices (KRW 3–7 million), crude design and limited distribution centred on department stores. Bodyfriend broke through on three fronts: whole product, sales channel and pricing.

For what one massage outside costs, the whole family can use a massage chair for a month.

Rental pricing. In 2010 Bodyfriend became the first in the industry to introduce rental. Noting that a full-body massage costs about KRW 50,000, it offered a 39-month rental at KRW 49,500 a month. That Bodyfriend's very first patent was not a product technology but a rental sales method ("Method of providing a rental service for installed products," filed 2012) shows how strategically it pursued rental. Revenue, which was KRW 5 billion in 2009 before rental, grew rapidly to KRW 18.9 billion in 2010 and KRW 34.1 billion in 2011.

Design innovation (whole product). Early products followed Japanese design, but with the design R&D centre established in 2013 the company broke away from crude styling and a limited palette. Ergonomic design inspired by first-class airline seats delivered comfort, and design filings reached 257 between 2013 and 2022.

Experience spaces (sales channel). Distribution centred on department stores could not offer the experience of use widely enough. In 2014 Bodyfriend partnered with a coffee franchise to open "Café de Bodyfriend," a café-style experience space, and converted its directly operated showrooms into "Bodyfriend Lounges," optimising the customer experience environment. Letting customers try the product in a relaxed setting removed resistance and widened the consumer base.

4.5 The significance of the technology and management strategies

Bodyfriend has held revenue in the KRW 500 billion range over the past three years, opening a gap of more than three times over second-placed Bokjung Jehyung, and took first place in massage chair customer satisfaction for three consecutive years from 2020 to 2022. It scored well against competitors on detailed measures including massage performance, store facilities and environment, product design and colour, and range of functions — confirming that its strategies of strengthening healthcare functions through technology convergence, building experience-led distribution channels and innovating in design were effective.

Implications
  • Synergy between technology and management strategy: From its early years the company pursued technology convergence through high R&D investment and a differentiated research organisation including recruited medical specialists, strengthening healthcare functions; and it crossed the chasm in the massage chair market itself through design innovation, rental sales and experience spaces. The combination of the two strategies drove its market leadership.
  • Why the Japanese firms fell behind: Japanese companies with 70 years of manufacturing history concentrated their R&D on A61H (massage function), confining the massage chair to a device that only massages, and neglected design and functional innovation while holding to a high-price strategy suited to an ageing, conservative customer base — and so fell behind in the Korean market.
  • What makes a game changer: Even a leader with a long manufacturing history can be pushed out at any time if it simply repeats its existing R&D and fails to read consumption trends, while a latecomer that reads market change accurately and pursues R&D from a fresh perspective can become a game changer. That said, the market contraction since 2023 and intensifying competition as large firms such as LG Electronics and SK Magic enter warrant continued observation.
