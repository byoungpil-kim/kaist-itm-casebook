A Successful Digital Transformation Strategy in Manufacturing: The Case of HD Hyundai Infracore

Manufacturing accounts for 27.8% of Korea's GDP, which makes strengthening the sector's digital foundation more important than anything else. Yet the level of digital transformation in manufacturing is relatively low compared with non-manufacturing. This study analyses the success factors at HD Hyundai Infracore — the first company in Korea to adopt Palantir's big data platform and a case that has drawn attention as a digital transformation success — and, in particular, empirically demonstrates the effect of gatekeepers on an organisation's digital transformation.

Research summary
  • Manufacturing accounts for a very large share of Korea's economy, so strengthening its digital foundation is essential; yet the level of digital transformation in manufacturing is relatively low compared with non-manufacturing, and research to promote it is called for.
  • This study analyses the success factors in digital transformation at HD Hyundai Infracore, a case among Korean manufacturers that has drawn attention as a success.
  • Organisational factors played the decisive role in that success: strong leadership, a change in organisational culture, a dedicated organisation and developing gatekeepers. In particular, the strategy of spreading agile working methods and a data-driven decision culture to all employees through developing gatekeepers contributed greatly.
  • Gatekeepers affect differences in the level of digital transformation across organisations: the digital absorptive capacity, number and level of digital transformation of gatekeepers within an organisation all showed a significant positive relationship with the level of digital transformation among staff.
  • Against a background of little research on the role of gatekeepers in digital transformation, the contribution of this study is to demonstrate that contribution empirically through a single company case, offering lessons for manufacturers preparing for digital transformation.

1. Introduction

Despite the high level of interest in digital transformation, Korea's own level remains low. According to a survey by the Korea Industrial Technology Association, only 9.7% of Korean firms are actively pursuing digital transformation, and including those pursuing it partially (20.9%) the figure reaches just 30.6%. In a survey of mid-sized firms, 49.8% of respondents rated their level of response at stage 1 and 32.5% at stage 0, giving an average of 0.9, while only 1.2% reported stage 4 (maturity).

HD Hyundai Infracore, transferred from the Doosan Group to the HD Hyundai Group in 2021, produces and sells construction equipment and engines. It was the first company in Korea to adopt Palantir's big data platform and has drawn attention as a digital transformation success. Building on that success, the HD Hyundai Group is preparing to establish a joint venture with Palantir and is rolling the platform out across all its affiliates.

2. Theoretical background

Digital transformation

Gong and Ribiere (2021) define digital transformation as "a fundamental change process, enabled by the use of innovative digital technologies, grounded in the strategic use of core resources and capabilities." Unlike digitization, which converts analogue information into digital form, and digitalization, which focuses on incremental improvement at the operational level, digital transformation entails fundamental and radical change and demands new structures, culture and strategy. Its success factors divide into technological, organisational and environmental. Kane's (2019) research finds that the answer does not lie in technology: organisations that are not agile and innovative, and cultures that cannot change, are the heart of the problem. Research on digital transformation in manufacturing (Vogelsang et al., 2018) likewise found organisational factors to be the most important.

Gatekeepers

New information enters an organisation through the contact and communication of its engineers with individuals outside it. But not every engineer is good at communicating externally. Within an organisation there are a few key people on whom other members depend heavily, and Thomas J. Allen defined them as "technological gatekeepers." New information enters the organisation through gatekeepers and spreads to other members through the gatekeeper network. From the perspective of Cohen and Levinthal's (1990) absorptive capacity, gatekeepers monitor the external environment continuously and convey effectively information that internal staff find hard to understand, contributing to the absorption, mediation and diffusion of knowledge. Without that role, an organisation cannot learn of its own accord and its innovation process inevitably stalls.

3. Digital transformation at HD Hyundai Infracore

Digital strategy

To respond pre-emptively to the limits and risks facing a traditional manufacturer, HD Hyundai Infracore set three digital strategies. First, digitalising the business — creating new businesses through equipment electrification and integrated construction site control solutions. Second, digitalising operations — securing data-driven decision-making in product development, sales and production. Third, digitalising the way people work — spreading a culture of making decisions based on data.

Choosing a big data platform

The biggest obstacle was data silos. Vast amounts of data had accumulated across the company, but data did not flow smoothly between systems, making it hard to derive meaningful insight. After contacting dozens of firms from 2018, HD Hyundai Infracore ultimately chose Palantir. At the time Palantir was a unicorn attracting attention in the United States but a relatively unfamiliar foreign company in Korea. Unable to invest immediately in the solution of an unfamiliar start-up, HD Hyundai Infracore visited Palantir's London office to see best practice at existing customers first-hand, including Airbus's Skywise big data platform, and signed a strategic partnership in April 2019.

Building the big data platform

After the partnership was signed, a "Square TF team" was formed — the idea being to gather data in the square (the platform) and grow collective intelligence in proportion to the square. Beginning with supply chain management (SCM) and field claim and quality management (FCQM), the team extended in 2020 into procurement, aftermarket, R&D and production, bringing in business experts so that operational needs would be reflected effectively.

The Square TF team's digital transformation started not from data but from decisions — an approach Palantir calls "transformation backwards."

The team first identified the decisions needed and defined the business problems to be solved, prioritised them by business impact, and only then defined the data required. Using Palantir's Foundry, it built data pipelines (data lineage) connecting, processing, transforming and integrating data from source systems. The resulting big data platform, combining data intelligence with 360-degree analysis, was rolled out company-wide in 2020 under the name DI360.

Using the platform: best practice

Employees highlighted three advantages of DI360. First, the inefficiency of logging into separate systems by domain to gather data was removed, dramatically shortening the time needed to prepare reports. Second, a single equipment serial number gives ready access to production, sales and customer usage information. Third, setting up the logic once means reports update automatically in real time, so anyone can use the data to produce analytical reports carrying their own insight.

Two examples of best practice stand out. The first used telematics management system (TMS) data to analyse how field customers use their machines and applied that to new product development. GPS and TMS data revealed that about half of the 21-tonne wheeled excavators sold in Europe were operating in sugar beet fields in north-eastern France, and that maximum power mode was in use 50% of the time. The next-generation 21-tonne wheeled excavator for Europe was accordingly developed with an emphasis on improved maximum power performance. The second calculated, from SCM and TMS data, the carbon emissions of the product use phase — 87.5% of total emissions across the construction equipment value chain — and on that basis built a carbon reduction technology roadmap (TRM) centred on medium and large excavators.

4. Why the digital transformation succeeded

Strong leadership

HD Hyundai Infracore had no separate CDO role; then-CEO Son Dong-yeon led the digital transformation directly, and the head of the strategy and digital division — the son of then-chairman Park Yong-maan — had strong backing. Choosing Palantir, little known in Korea, as a partner and committing KRW 8 billion in investment both rested on that leadership. After DI360 launched, CEO Son directed executives in each division to find and execute digital transformation projects, and had the executive responsible answer questions directly at project result presentations, drawing the leaders of each organisation into active participation.

Change in organisational culture

A McKinsey survey identified organisational culture as the greatest obstacle to success in the digital era. Through its collaboration with Palantir, HD Hyundai Infracore gradually absorbed an agile working culture. The organisation builds and validates data pipelines and workflows quickly using small datasets, then expands projects through cycles of revision that incorporate feedback — a tendency resembling the lean startup's build–measure–learn feedback loop. This working culture carried over unchanged into business roll-out projects after the initial project ended, helping the whole organisation learn to work in an agile way.

A dedicated organisation

Against a reality in which only 8.2% of mid-sized firms have an organisation pursuing digital transformation and 76.2% report having no staff for it at all, HD Hyundai Infracore runs a dedicated Data Intelligence team. Converted from the Square TF team into a permanent team after DI360 launched, it handles both platform operations and the change management that shifts working methods and culture across the company. It designated one day a month as a training day for various programmes, and in 2021 ran a "DDD (Data-Driven Decision) Company" campaign, carrying out 24 pilot projects over the year and recognising six as best practice. As a result, average monthly DI360 usage rose from 767 hours in 2020 to 1,364 hours in 2022, and average monthly users doubled from 163 to 325.

Developing data agents (developing gatekeepers)

HD Hyundai Infracore called the people corresponding to Gartner's "citizen data scientist" data agents, and they performed a role much like the gatekeeper in innovation research. Twenty-four data agents were developed through the DDD Company campaign, with the Data Intelligence team providing intensive basic and advanced training over a year. Data agents used DI360 themselves and passed on the experience of the projects they ran to their own teams. Where the Data Intelligence team acted as gatekeeper between the outside world and the organisation, data agents acted as gatekeepers spreading knowledge and culture among sub-units within it.

HD Hyundai Infracore worked to spread the agile working methods and data-driven decision culture it had learned from Palantir — starting with the Data Intelligence team, then data agents, and ultimately all employees — and this strategy contributed greatly to the success of its digital transformation.

5. The effect of gatekeepers on digital transformation

A survey of DI360 usage by team in 2022 found that average weekly usage among members of teams with a gatekeeper was significantly higher than among those without (t = 1.847, p = 0.033). Because usage also differed among teams that had gatekeepers, three hypotheses were set to analyse that variation. H1: a gatekeeper's digital absorptive capacity is positively related to the level of digital transformation among staff. H2: the number of gatekeepers is positively related to the level of digital transformation among staff. H3: a gatekeeper's own level of digital transformation is positively related to the level of digital transformation among staff.

The analysis of 2022 DI360 usage data covered 20 teams that had gatekeepers. Weekly DI360 usage by staff was set as the dependent variable, with a gatekeeper's capability (project evaluation score of 1–3), the number of gatekeepers and their weekly usage as independent variables, and team function as a control. Multiple regression was run in Stata. All three independent variables had a statistically significant positive effect on the dependent variable, so all three hypotheses were supported. By size of coefficient, the effects ran in the order of number of gatekeepers, absorptive capacity, and level of digital transformation.

6. Conclusion

HD Hyundai Infracore's successful digital transformation rests on organisational factors — strong leadership, a change in organisational culture, a dedicated organisation and the development of gatekeepers — and the study verified quantitatively the effect of gatekeepers on differences in the level of digital transformation across organisations. This is consistent with earlier research finding that innovative IT use is led by gatekeepers. One limitation remains: given the nature of internal company data, demographic information on users such as academic field and highest level of education could not be used as variables.

Implications
  • Emphasise organisational factors: Technological factors alone cannot deliver the full benefit of digital transformation. Firms should secure digital leadership, with senior management such as a CEO or CDO leading the transformation, and build a dedicated organisation to carry out change management that shifts working methods and culture as well as providing technical support.
  • Use people from inside the organisation: Seventy per cent of failed digital transformations stem from employee resistance. Involving internal experts who understand the organisation's culture and the heart of its decision-making leads the transformation to be applied in a form optimised for actual work.
  • Develop gatekeepers within each organisation: The more gatekeepers an organisation has and the higher their digital absorptive capacity, the higher its level of digital transformation. Systematic, intensive training should build them in both number and quality, and after they are developed a dedicated organisation must keep monitoring and supporting them so that they continue producing output.
