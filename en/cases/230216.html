Innovation Strategy of a Bio-Platform Company: The Case of Halozyme

Halozyme Therapeutics is a global bio-platform company with only about 140 employees. Even so, its net margin reached 90% in 2021, and Goldman Sachs ranked it first among listed companies as a high-growth, high-profit business able to withstand rising interest rates. This case study analyses the core capabilities and innovation strategies through which Halozyme — holder of the ENHANZE technology that converts intravenous (IV) drugs into subcutaneous (SC) formulations — became the world's leading high-growth, ultra-profitable company.

Research summary
  • In the bio industry, a platform technology is a base technology that can be applied to existing drugs to derive new forms of medicine.
  • As competition in new drug development intensifies with the expiry of major global patents, bio companies are turning to "platform" technologies that preserve existing efficacy while allowing unlimited extension.
  • This study analyses the innovation case of Halozyme, a global bio-platform company holding technology that converts IV formulations into SC ones, examining its core capabilities together with the innovation strategies — architectural innovation and open innovation — behind its exceptional market competitiveness.
  • From this it seeks to share the value of the platform business and to suggest directions for innovation that firms can draw on to develop their competitiveness.

1. Background and purpose

Global manufacturing operating margins run at about 5% in Korea and only about 9% worldwide. Outside semiconductors, major manufacturing industries earn low net profit relative to revenue, which limits growth funded from their own investment, and when external shocks such as rate rises or a pandemic compound the problem they risk losing the industry's competitiveness altogether. Analysing innovation cases at financially robust firms is therefore an important task.

Halozyme, founded in 1998 and listed on NASDAQ in 2004, is based in San Diego, California, and its main business is the bio platform. As of 2023 its market capitalisation was about KRW 6.5 trillion with roughly 140 employees — maximum output from minimal human resources. Revenue roughly tripled over the three years from 2019, and of total revenue of about KRW 830 billion in 2022, royalties accounted for about KRW 450 billion. Its net margin reached about 90% in 2021.

The bio platform and the hyaluronidase market

A bio platform is a base technology that can be applied to existing drugs to derive multiple candidate substances by varying the protein target. In architectural terms it acts as a tool implementing a drug's sub-function in a modular architecture, where product functions map one-to-one onto components. The bio-platform industry is a market Halozyme itself created and grew: by attaching a suitable platform to a bestselling drug, big pharma can develop a new medicine at low cost and low risk.

Halozyme's flagship product family, ENHANZE, is a hyaluronidase. The hyaluronidase market is growing at about 9% a year, and on a human hyaluronidase basis is expected to grow from KRW 600 billion in 2018 to about KRW 1.5 trillion by 2027. Halozyme is the global number one with a commanding share of this market, and the only company since to enter this monopoly market is Korea's Alteogen.

2. Core capabilities

2.2 World-leading bioengineering technology

ENHANZE is a substance created by cutting and joining parts of Hyal1 and PH20, proteins that break down hyaluronic acid (HA) in the skin, into a single molecule. By dissolving hyaluronic acid in skin tissue it opens space for an SC-formulated drug to pass through, acting as a drug delivery platform that delivers the drug into the body at an appropriate concentration and rate. Cutting the domains of small protein molecules and joining them together is regarded as protein engineering of the highest difficulty.

Ordinary antibody therapeutics have molecular weights above 150 kDa, too large to pass through hyaluronic acid when injected subcutaneously; adding the sub-function of ENHANZE — hyaluronic acid degradation by what is known as rHuPH20 — makes conversion to an SC formulation possible. The end-customer value of that conversion is clear. Conventional intravenous biologics take anywhere from an hour to 20 hours to administer, but reformulated with ENHANZE they can be given in around five minutes, and self-administration becomes possible, saving additional medical costs such as hospitalisation.

2.3 Unmatched patent knowledge and know-how in bio platforms

Halozyme has filed some 478 patents, of which a large share relate to rHuPH20 (human hyaluronidase). It has built patent barriers by type — manufacturing, use, formulation — blocking competitors at source. Patent network analysis (using WIPS and Gephi) shows Halozyme's patents ranking second overall in forward citations with very high betweenness centrality, confirming a structure in which filing a patent covering both an antibody and hyaluronidase is effectively impossible without citing Halozyme's.

In other words, Halozyme's unmatched patent position functions as a barrier to new entrants, because anyone entering the hyaluronidase market must pass through its patents.

3. Innovation strategy 1: a modular architecture platform business

In Professor Fujimoto's architectural positioning framework, the most powerful type is Integral Inside, Modular Outside (IIMO): internally the firm holds a black box hard to imitate thanks to its independent design capability, while externally the product is easy to use without major adjustment for each customer, giving high extendability. Halozyme's ENHANZE bio platform is precisely this type.

As the patents on many global mega-drugs launched since the 2000s expired, pharmaceutical companies needed to secure "additional value" or an innovative patent extension strategy to hold market share. Halozyme pursued a modular platform sales and co-development partnership strategy, combining its patented SC-conversion substance ENHANZE with existing drugs of proven efficacy — attaching ENHANZE to Roche's Herceptin IV to develop Herceptin SC, or applying it to Janssen's Darzalex IV to develop Darzalex SC.

The strength of this model lies in minimising clinical failure risk and cost. Fewer than 30% of drugs pass from phase II to phase III, at a cost of at least KRW 100 billion, whereas ENHANZE converts an already commercialised drug into an SC formulation without changing the active pharmaceutical ingredient, so the risk of failure for lack of efficacy is very low. And because it is modular outside, a new contract is possible for every candidate in each partner's vast pipeline — 144 at Roche, 143 at BMS, 94 at Johnson & Johnson — giving high extendability even under exclusive agreements.

4. Innovation strategy 2: a royalty-centred revenue model built on technology licensing

Most bio companies run at a loss because developing a new drug takes an average of 15 years and more than KRW 1 trillion; about 80% of NASDAQ-listed biopharmaceutical companies record losses. Halozyme too faced the risk of an operating loss as R&D spending rose between 2014 and 2019, and in November 2019 it took the decision to halt in-house development of PEGPH20, a pancreatic cancer therapy it had invested in for more than a decade. Closing the oncology business, it cut more than 55% of its workforce (160 people) and reduced related costs by about KRW 150 billion as of 2020.

In its place the company built a milestone and royalty revenue model concentrated on licensing out ENHANZE. Technology licensing typically comprises a non-refundable up-front payment of 5–7%, milestones by clinical stage (about 10% at phase I, 15–20% at phase II, 20–25% at phase III) and royalties on sales after launch (about 40–50%). Between 2006 and 2022 Halozyme signed licensing agreements with ten global pharmaceutical companies covering some 60 substances, with total contract value estimated at around KRW 7 trillion.

Because this revenue arises not from further investment but from partners' clinical progress, it delivers maximum returns on minimum investment. Royalty income from Wave 1–4 ENHANZE products is expected to exceed KRW 1.3 trillion in 2027, with new revenue from Wave 5 expected in 2031.

5. Innovation strategy 3: strengthening competitiveness through open innovation

ENHANZE made SC conversion possible, but because an appropriate volume must be injected over a set period, self-administration still had limits. In August 2022 Halozyme pursued an open innovation strategy that absorbed capability while reducing its own R&D burden, acquiring Antares Pharma — holder of best-in-class auto-injector technology — for a total of USD 960 million (about KRW 1.25 trillion).

Converting IV to SC with ENHANZE and then combining Antares' auto-injection platform, the company developed new products easy to self-administer, and is running trials to cut auto-injection time from the current three minutes to 30 seconds. It sold 8 million units in 2022, the year of the acquisition, expanding into specialty pharmaceuticals and strengthening its positioning as an integrated bio drug delivery platform company.

Implications
  • Integral core technology plus a modular platform: With world-leading protein engineering and patent capability, Halozyme created a blue ocean in bio platforms and, in an IIMO architecture, generated continuing contracts on a foundation of high extendability. When the sub-function of a modular product is superior enough to determine the competitiveness of the finished product's main function, adoption becomes essential — establishing a powerful and secure business model.
  • Patent barriers and revenue model innovation: Patent barriers blocked followers from entering the market, and after halting in-house development the company shifted to a royalty-centred revenue model built on technology transfer, maximising net profit stage by stage on a continuing basis.
  • Extending to other industries: Designing and developing differentiated integral core technology together with modular platform products that apply it generally is vital to securing technological competitiveness for both firms and countries. Adding open innovation that grows the market and a strategy that blocks followers makes a sustainable market leader.
