Average daily smoking rates across 37 major countries fell from 20.6% in 2011 to 15.9% in 2021 — a generational shift toward a smoke-free world. Yet KT&G, Korea's largest tobacco manufacturer, is posting record results even in these conditions, and in the heated tobacco market it entered as a latecomer before overtaking global rivals to take the leading market share in both 2022 and 2023. This study analyzes, from a management-of-technology perspective, the background to KT&G's growth and its innovation strategy.

Research summary
  • Since the harms of tobacco were scientifically established in the mid-twentieth century, national regulation and anti-smoking campaigns have tightened, and average daily smoking rates across 37 major OECD countries fell from 20.6% in 2011 to 15.9% in 2021 — a generational shift toward a smoke-free world.
  • Despite these conditions, KT&G, Korea's largest tobacco manufacturer, is posting record results, and it overtook global rivals to take the leading share of Korea's heated tobacco market in both 2022 and 2023.
  • Analyzed from a management-of-technology perspective, KT&G's continued growth rests on the niche entry of the ultra-slim, low-tar brand ESSE, the opening of global markets, and a successful paradigm shift to heated tobacco — with the common denominator being the minimization of negative externalities.
  • Reaching first place in heated tobacco as a latecomer rested on a fast follower strategy that unlocked the pioneer's consumer lock-in, appropriability secured through patent innovation, and the successful commercialization of core technology.

1. Introduction — background and purpose

KT&G was founded in 1987 as the state-owned Korea Monopoly Corporation, renamed Korea Tobacco & Ginseng Corporation in 1989 and privatized in December 2002. As of 2023, tobacco accounts for KRW 3.6 trillion (61.6%) of total revenue of KRW 5.9 trillion — the company's main business. Despite the adverse environment surrounding smoking, in 2023 it achieved consolidated revenue of KRW 5.9 trillion and operating profit of KRW 1.2 trillion, and the compound annual growth rate of tobacco revenue over the past six years, at 8.66%, is faster than in the past. That growth is closely tied to the heated tobacco (heat-not-burn, HNB) business. KT&G launched lil SOLID only in November 2017, after Philip Morris's IQOS in May and BAT's glo in August, making it a latecomer in the Korean market — yet within five years it took the leading market share at 47.5%.

2. Theoretical background — industry analysis and Pavitt's taxonomy

Consuming tobacco products necessarily carries negative externalities. Smoking harms the health not only of the smoker but of those exposed to second-hand smoke, and governments have tightened regulation through taxation, mandatory graphic warnings and the expansion of smoke-free areas. Taken together, the literature suggests that tobacco manufacturers need to target overseas markets where regulation is comparatively loose, to pre-empt the electronic cigarette market with its smaller negative externalities relative to combustible cigarettes, and to innovate in products that reduce odour and harm.

The global tobacco market was worth about USD 850 billion in 2020, with combustible cigarettes accounting for 84.1%; but over the four years 2017–2020, compound annual growth ran at 53.5% for heated tobacco and 23.1% for e-liquid products against 2.7% for combustibles — a striking rise for electronic products. In Korea, heated tobacco expanded rapidly from 2.3% of sales volume in 2017 to 16.9% in 2023. KT&G holds about 66% of Korea's combustible market and about 47% of heated tobacco; with global key players present in the market, KT&G is almost the only domestic tobacco company anywhere in the world maintaining a share above 60%.

In Pavitt's taxonomy, the combustible cigarette industry is supplier-dominated: its technological trajectory is process innovation and its appropriability regime is determined by non-technological factors such as trademarks and marketing. The heated tobacco industry, by contrast, is science-based, with the electrical heating device at its core, so technological innovation through R&D and appropriability secured through patent filings both matter.

3. Case analysis

3.1 Historical timeline — transformation, global expansion, paradigm shift

The greatest change in the transformation period (1987–2005) was the full opening of Korea's tobacco market in 1988. Global tobacco companies threatened the domestic market with formidable capital and brand recognition, and with privatization at the end of 2002 on top of that, KT&G — still strongly marked by its state-enterprise DNA — faced a threat to its survival. In the global expansion period (2006–2016), as regulation intensified through the widening of smoke-free areas (2012), a KRW 2,000 price increase (2015) and mandatory warning text (2016), the company pushed hard into overseas markets, building plants in Turkey (2008), Iran (2009) and Russia (2010) and acquiring Trisakti in Indonesia (2011). In the paradigm shift period (2017 to the present), the main business has been moving from combustibles to HNB, and the HNB business grew rapidly at a CAGR of 35% between 2018 and 2023.

3.2 Growth strategy — niche, global, paradigm shift

Entering a niche. About 96% of the global combustible market is regular-type cigarettes 7.8 mm in diameter, a segment already dominated by the global majors and judged realistically uncontestable. KT&G therefore attacked a niche in 1996 with ESSE, an ultra-slim cigarette 5.4 mm in diameter. What sets it apart is that it was not merely a thin cigarette but combined low-tar technology. ESSE Classic delivered 6.5 mg of tar per stick in 1996, but by 2007 ESSE Soo 0.1 had cut this to 0.1 mg, and smell-care technologies followed — a breath odour reduction capsule (ESSE Change, 2013) and a hand odour reduction filter (ESSE Change Frozen, 2020). Building a brand image that thin cigarettes are smoother, milder and less harmful, ESSE has long held first place in Korea with around 30% share and also leads the global ultra-slim market with 36%.

Opening global markets. Here too the niche strategy proved effective. Avoiding regions dominated by the global big three (Philip Morris, BAT and JT), the company targeted emerging markets in the Middle East, Russia and Southeast Asia, weighing not only tobacco demand but also cultural characteriztics, consumer trends and diplomatic relations in choosing its entry points. Within ten years of the start of exports in 2001, ESSE was selling more than 20 billion sticks a year and had become the leading brand chosen by one in three ultra-slim smokers worldwide. Overseas revenue rose from 16% of total tobacco revenue in 2007 to 38% in 2022 — a shift from a domestic monopoly to a global tobacco company.

The paradigm shift to heated tobacco. A conventional cigarette burns at 600–900°C, generating smoke containing harmful chemicals; HNB heats the stick at 250–350°C, generating an aerosol free of combustion by-products with less ash and odour. KT&G diversified its portfolio with lil SOLID (2017), lil HYBRID (2018) and lil ABLE (2022), and revenue from this next-generation product (NGP) business grew from KRW 176.3 billion in 2018 to KRW 779.4 billion in 2023, a six-year CAGR of 34.6%, establishing it as a new growth engine. It went further, signing overseas sales agreements with Philip Morris in 2020 and 2023 (a 15-year long-term contract) under which Philip Morris sells KT&G's smoke-free products exclusively in global markets outside Korea. The agreement is estimated to deliver a 20.6% CAGR in overseas NGP revenue over the next 15 years.

3.3 Innovation strategy — fast follower, patent innovation, commercialization

Fast follower. HNB products require the purchase of a device costing around KRW 100,000, so switching costs are high, and the pioneer Philip Morris built consumer lock-in: buy an IQOS once and you must keep buying its dedicated HEETS sticks. Latecomer KT&G's lil SOLID integrated the battery and heating element into a single unit, allowing continuous smoking (more than 20 sticks on one charge) where IQOS required a five-minute recharge, and it worked not only with its own FIIT sticks but also with IQOS's HEETS, lowering switching costs. Reviewed as "combining the best of IQOS and glo," it unlocked the pioneer's lock-in. The company then launched 11 new products in total between 2017 and 2023 — against Philip Morris's seven and BAT's nine — minimizing lead time and pre-empting consumer needs.

KT&G unlocked the pioneer's consumer lock-in through product innovation and minimized switching costs, and then pursued a fast follower strategy of launching a wider range of products more quickly than its competitors.

Patent innovation. In Utterback's pattern of innovation, the electronic cigarette industry is still before a dominant design has emerged — a fluid phase with many competitors, high product variety and surging patent filings, that is, a stage of product innovation. KT&G's patent filings jumped from 317 in the five years 2014–2018 (around ninth place) to 3,176 in the five years 2019–2023, the most of any company, ahead of leaders such as Philip Morris. Its share of electronic cigarette patents now ranks first in Korea (21.72%) and second globally (6.11%). Its technological impact (CPP), however, is below average, at 2.2 domestically (overall average 5.2) and 1.65 globally (overall average 4.44).

Commercializing the technology. Analyzing core patents by the ratio of citations received to citations made, the highest score of 39 went to an "electric heater for heated tobacco" filed in 2005, long before the company launched any electronic cigarette. Examples of commercializing core patents include: ① lil HYBRID 1.0, the first in the industry to add a liquid cartridge, reducing the steamed odour and bringing vapour volume up to the level of a combustible cigarette (patent filed November 2017, productized about a year later in December 2018); ② lil HYBRID 2.0, the first in the industry to implement "Smart On" automatic preheating through a stick insertion sensor; and ③ lil ABLE Premium (launched November 2022), which connects to a smartphone over Bluetooth to display calls, texts and app notifications.

4. Findings

The answer to RQ1 (continued growth) has three parts: ① successful branding of ESSE in the ultra-slim, low-tar niche; ② opening global markets (overseas revenue of KRW 1.0 trillion in 2022, 37.8% of the total); and ③ a successful paradigm shift to heated tobacco. The answer to RQ2 (a latecomer reaching first place) also has three: ① a fast follower strategy that unlocked the pioneer's lock-in through an integrated battery architecture and HEETS compatibility; ② appropriability secured through 3,176 patent filings over the past five years; and ③ successful commercialization of core technology.

5. Conclusion

Tobacco is regarded, alongside alcohol and gambling, as a classic sin industry, and the shift to a smoke-free world is accelerating — as the UK's proposed smoking ban on anyone born after 2009 illustrates. Even so, KT&G continues to grow, projecting revenue of KRW 10.2 trillion by 2027.

Implications
  • It succeeded in minimizing negative externalities. The niche entry of the ultra-slim, low-tar ESSE and the paradigm shift to heated tobacco are the leading examples. Against the headwinds of regulation and falling smoking rates, product innovation directed at reducing harm and odour was the common denominator of its growth.
  • It succeeded as a fast follower. It unlocked the pioneer's consumer lock-in through product innovation (the integrated battery) and minimized switching costs (HEETS compatibility), then worked to secure the dominant design by minimizing new product lead times.
  • It succeeded in securing appropriability and commercializing technology. In an electronic cigarette industry still in its fluid phase, patent innovation gave it exclusive protection for the returns on its R&D, and it connected core technology to product innovation meeting consumer needs.
  • Limitations: The study focuses on product innovation and does not address process innovation cases such as cost reduction and automation, and it analyzes appropriability mainly through patents, so further research is needed on trademarks, designs, trade secrets and know-how.
