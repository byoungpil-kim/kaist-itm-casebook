The 2016 Gyeongju earthquake (magnitude 5.8) and the 2017 Pohang earthquake (magnitude 5.4) caused 29,393 cases of non-structural elements such as ceiling materials falling, prompting the government to make seismic design of non-structural elements mandatory. The structural gap in this market — one that neither the large incumbents nor the small firms that had developed technology could fill — was filled by Hwayoung, a latecomer SME founded in 2011. This case study examines how a resource-constrained latecomer SME turned into the industry's rule maker in the face of a government-induced demand pull shock, through an integrated framework of architectural innovation, dynamic capabilities and the technological innovation system (TIS).

Research summary
  • Through the case of Hwayoung, this study examines how a resource-constrained latecomer SME can turn into the industry's rule maker in the face of a government-induced demand pull shock.
  • The analysis is conducted through an integrated framework of architectural innovation, dynamic capabilities and the technological innovation system (TIS).
  • Hwayoung closed the technological gap through geometry innovation, used the double sensing arising from an integrated manufacturing-and-installation structure to respond pre-emptively to the demand shock, and led industry standards by actively activating TIS system functions through strategic links with four sets of actors: research institutes, government, large construction firms and large materials firms.
  • The study demonstrates that innovation-oriented public procurement functions as an institutional accelerator enabling a latecomer SME to reverse its market position, and presents theoretical and policy implications.

1. Introduction — an inflection point and a structural gap

Korea's light-gauge steel ceiling market reached a structural inflection point in the mid-2010s as several shocks overlapped in a short period. First, the Gyeongju and Pohang earthquakes caused 29,393 cases of non-structural elements falling, in response to which the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (2019) and the Ministry of Education (2020) made seismic design mandatory. Second, as of 2025 some 28,216 school buildings — 43.4% of those more than 30 years old — are ageing. Third, as a measure against asbestos, a class 1 carcinogen, the government is pursuing complete replacement of asbestos ceiling materials in educational facilities by 2027. Fourth, requirements for non-combustible materials are being tightened in stages. These changes simultaneously exposed three structural limits of existing technology: the hazard of asbestos tex as a material, the seismic vulnerability of the C-type carrying channel and double M-bar clip method, and the variation in installation quality arising from dependence on subcontractors.

The market demanded a standardised system satisfying seismic resistance, non-combustibility and ease of installation at once, but no one filled the gap. Large incumbents with revenue from KRW 30 billion to KRW 200 billion — Yuchang, Daehan Steel, Daeryung, Shinsung Gangeon — rest on a strategic rigidity optimised for mass-producing standard sections. The smaller incumbents that had developed technology, meanwhile, failed at commercialisation for four reasons: insufficient performance reliability, installation complexity, the absence of a certification strategy and inadequate commercialisation strategy. Some 40 to 50 companies are registered as seismic ceiling suppliers on the national procurement system, but only around ten have meaningful installation records.

The subject: Hwayoung as the answer

Hwayoung, which filled that gap, recorded revenue of KRW 20.2 billion in 2025, including KRW 11.5 billion through public procurement. It developed Korea's first asbestos-free seismic ceiling panel system and was designated by the Public Procurement Service as an excellent product — the sole such designation in the gypsum tex finishing market. It also recorded the industry's highest seismic performance at 1.88 g, up to 88% above competitors, and achieved a 46% cost reduction against the Ministry of Land's standard unit price schedule for the first half of 2026. Hwayoung's share of the seismic ceiling procurement market (about KRW 250 billion) is estimated at 15–18%, and in the specific category of gypsum-tex-based seismic excellent products it holds a dominant share above 70%.

2. Theoretical background — an integrated framework of three theories

The study analyses Hwayoung's multi-layered innovation mechanism through three theories. Architectural innovation (Henderson and Clark, 1990) addresses what was innovated: innovation that retains the core concepts of components while reorganising how they connect and how the system is designed. Dynamic capabilities (Teece, 2007) addresses how decisions were made and acted on, explaining the meta-capabilities of sensing, seizing and transforming. The technological innovation system (Carlsson and Stankiewicz, 1991; Hekkert et al., 2007) addresses with whom and through what structure the innovation happened, examining how the system functions of actors, institutions and networks are activated within a specific technology sector. The study conceptualises the phenomenon whereby an exogenous event — an earthquake — creates abrupt market demand through institutional intervention as a "government-induced demand pull shock," and sets as its central analytical question: faced with the same demand shock, why was it Hwayoung alone that could grasp it faster than anyone and respond in an organised way?

3. Methodology

Following Yin's (2018) single case study design, the study triangulates multiple sources: an in-depth interview with CEO Park Bong-je and Hwayoung's internal materials (primary sources); the national procurement system, the Korean Intellectual Property Office, government policy documents and press coverage (secondary sources); and relevant academic literature. Analysis applied theory-driven pattern matching, aiming at analytical rather than statistical generalisation.

4. The Hwayoung case

Company overview and a three-stage growth trajectory

Hwayoung's starting point lies in the 25 years of field experience founder Park Bong-je accumulated at a large PVC window manufacturer between 1987 and 2011. The company's growth trajectory took shape through three qualitative transitions. In the first stage (2011–2014) it moved from distributing to manufacturing PVC window reinforcement; in the second (2015–2019) it focused on specialising in light-gauge steel and on disciplined management; and in the third (2020–present) it pre-empted a technology-specialised niche and turned into a rule maker in procurement. Revenue grew from KRW 2.38 billion in 2012 to KRW 20.2 billion in 2025, and the company remained profitable throughout — including through the death valley period (2014–2016) and the commodity-product plateau (2019–2020). Gross profit margin in particular rebounded in a V shape, from 11.0% in 2023 to 19.7% in 2025, showing that the arena shift out of a red ocean of low-price bidding into a position with pricing power was financially complete.

Architectural innovation — redesigning the architecture through geometry innovation

Hwayoung's innovation concentrates on improving performance through mechanical structural change — "form innovation" — as an alternative to developing new chemical materials. It uses commodity materials already proven in the market, galvanised steel sheet and standard clips, and improves whole-system performance by optimising geometry and connection method. Hwayoung conceptualises this as "resource refusal": strategically avoiding the high-cost risk of new material development and redeploying existing resources instead. The approach begins in reverse innovation grounded in the field, arising from a company structure that holds both manufacturing and installation; field experience of clips coming loose, installation complexity and excessive material cost (tacit knowledge) is converted into drawings and patent specifications (explicit knowledge).

The double M-bar clip, the previous dominant design, was fixed to the carrying channel by hand pressure in a simple surface contact structure, and came loose easily under seismic vibration. Hwayoung's solution comprises two layers of redesign. First, S-channel geometry innovation: redesigning the C- and U-shaped sections as an S-shape increased structural strength 2.6-fold over the C-shape (KCL, 2022), creating a mechanical locking effect with the clip. Second, the Hwayoung seismic clip (design registration 30-1030167) wraps all three faces of the S-channel, fundamentally preventing detachment. With screw fastening and cross-coupling added as a double fastening system, the assembly achieved peak ground acceleration of 1.88 g in tests at the Pusan National University Seismic Simulation Center (2019) and on the KCL shaking table (ICC-ES AC156, 2022) — 57% above Mujin (1.20 g) and 88% above JNT (1.00 g), both also designated excellent products in procurement.

"Change the shape of the material so that only we can do it." — a deliberate choice to refuse the high-cost, high-risk path of materials innovation and concentrate capability on redesigning the connection structure.

Theoretically, Hwayoung's innovation counts as textbook architectural innovation: the core concepts of the components are retained — the clip fixes the channel, the channel bears the load, the material is galvanised steel sheet — while the connection structure is reorganised. Competitors that tried to change the connection structure by enlarging the section, by contrast, ended up raising cost and lowering performance through greater material input. Hwayoung also built a multi-lock structure through a patent mesh strategy overlaying three layers of protection — geometry (design rights), performance (structural patents) and process (installation method patents) — creating a causal ambiguity that makes it hard for competitors to reproduce the same performance even if they develop a similar shape.

Dynamic capabilities — changing the playing field

Hwayoung's dynamic capability appeared not as simple improvement of existing capability but as a replacement of the market in which it competes: moving the playing field from a commodity market of low entry barriers, price-based competition and large firms' volume advantage to a technology-intensive niche where technology, certification and patents act as barriers to entry. In Teece's frame, the founder's 25 years of prior knowledge (sensing) supported sequential expansion into adjacent fields — from PVC window fixings to PVC reinforcement, light-gauge steel, seismic ceiling systems and dry walls (seizing) — while the organisation was reorganised (transforming) through a dual production structure across the Icheon plant (2015) and the Hwaseong plant (2021) and the introduction of MES, WMS and QMS.

Hwayoung's sensing in particular worked along two paths. Institutional sensing picked up regulatory signals such as mandatory seismic design and asbestos replacement policy in advance, while prior-knowledge sensing drew on a mental map of the market built over 25 years in the field. Both pointed in the same direction — technology specialisation, certification-based, niche — which accelerated decision-making. Seizing was resource-leveraging, turning high certification barriers into barriers to entry against others, and transforming formed a compounding structure in which each transition became the foundation for the next. The result was Korea's first asbestos-free seismic ceiling panel system in 2023, designated an excellent product by the Public Procurement Service.

The technological innovation system — becoming the designer of an industry standard

In Hekkert et al.'s (2007) TIS model, the actors activating system functions are generally taken to be large firms or government; Hwayoung challenges that premise directly. Linking with four sets of actors — a research institute (KCL), government (the ministries of land, education and environment and the Public Procurement Service), a large construction firm (Woomi Construction) and large materials firms (KCC and LX Hausys) — it functioned not as a beneficiary of system functions but as their proactive activator.

  • Axis 1, KCL (securing legitimacy): shaking table testing (certified at 1.88 g), pursuit of NEP and NET certification, and joint patent development internalised technical legitimacy.
  • Axis 2, government (market formation): filing patents before the mandatory seismic design notice took effect turned regulation into a structural barrier pushing competitors out. Designation as an excellent product by the Public Procurement Service (allowing negotiated contracts and exemption from two-stage competitive bidding) delivered meaningful orders in the first year of entry, about a third of the three years usually required.
  • Axis 3, Woomi Construction (knowledge diffusion): a technology cooperation MOU in July 2024 and a joint patent on a dry wall installation method (10-2820120) created a structure of horizontal co-development, and the S-channel method was incorporated into the standard installation architecture at Woomi Lin apartment sites.
  • Axis 4, KCC and LX Hausys (positive externalities): inverting the top-down structure in which large firms set specifications and SMEs execute, Hwayoung realised bottom-up collaboration by proposing technical ideas into large firms' product roadmaps first. KCC's seismic ceiling frame dedicated to large-format gypsum tex was listed in KCC's official catalogue in March 2022.
"A large company did not benevolently pick a small one. The small company stepped forward as the designer of the ecosystem, filling in the missing piece of the large company's roadmap." — CEO Park Bong-je

For a latecomer SME to move from beneficiary to designer of a TIS ecosystem, four conditions must operate simultaneously rather than in sequence: ① securing technical legitimacy through accredited verification; ② forming a market by pre-empting regulatory change; ③ diffusing knowledge through bottom-up collaboration with large construction firms; and ④ creating positive externalities through networks with large materials firms. The results of each axis convert into an entry asset for the next, forming a compounding structure that creates sustainable competitive advantage. Procurement data confirm the effect: after Hwayoung's excellent product designation, competitors' seismic performance figures rose from 1.0 g in 2018 to above 1.5 g in 2023 — Hwayoung's technical standard pulling up performance across the industry.

5. Conclusion

Hwayoung's success is the result of geometry innovation (technology), the strategic use of regulatory change (strategy) and TIS-based ecosystem design (ecosystem) combining organically to create synergy rather than remaining separate achievements. The strategic rigidity of the large incumbents was answered through architectural innovation; the four commercialisation failures of the smaller incumbents were overcome by changing the playing field and turning certification into a strategic asset; and the structural entrenchment of the market gap was resolved through the active activation of four actor axes.

Implications
  • A change of thinking: Rather than competing on price in a red ocean, a firm should open a non-price market through architectural innovation that retains existing resources and changes the logic of connection, instead of high-risk materials development. Protecting a change of geometry through patents makes it possible to design a compounding revenue structure running through procurement exclusivity to industry standardisation.
  • Turning installation into R&D: Using the field that large firms find hard to reach as an R&D base, and building a structure in which field pain points feed back into product design, creates a barrier grounded in tacit knowledge that cannot be replicated.
  • Regulation as a window of opportunity: "A firm that waits for regulation responds to it; a firm that anticipates regulation makes the standard." A phase of regulatory transition can be a window of opportunity in which the resource gap with incumbents is temporarily neutralised.
  • Policy implications: The study demonstrates that the Public Procurement Service's excellent product scheme functions as an institutional accelerator through which a latecomer SME with innovation capability can reverse its market position. That said, this is a single case study, and analysing the outcomes of the NEP and NET certifications in progress and of expansion into Japan, Taiwan and Indonesia remains a task for future research.
