Korea's pharmaceutical and bio market keeps growing and its R&D pipeline keeps expanding, yet cases of a drug developed by a Korean pharmaceutical company succeeding in global markets remain rare. Daewoong Pharmaceutical's botulinum toxin product Nabota grew quickly to become the third-ranked product in the US market within three years of its official launch there in 2019. This case study analyses, through the lenses of absorptive capacity and open innovation, the innovation factors that allowed a drug developed by a traditional Korean pharmaceutical company to be commercialised successfully in an advanced market.

Research summary
  • Pharmaceutical and bio markets and R&D pipelines are growing steadily at home and abroad, yet cases of a drug developed by a Korean pharmaceutical company succeeding in global markets remain rare.
  • Daewoong Pharmaceutical's Nabota grew quickly to become the third-ranked product in the US market within three years of its official launch there in 2019.
  • This study analyses the innovation factors that allowed a Korean-developed drug to be commercialised successfully in global markets, through the lenses of absorptive capacity and open innovation.
  • From this it draws implications for Korea's pharmaceutical and bio industry.

1. Introduction — pipelines are growing, so why is global success so rare?

The global pharmaceutical market, worth USD 1,161.9 billion in 2020, is growing at 4–7% a year, and Korea's new drug pipeline grew more than 2.5-fold from 573 in 2018 to 1,477 in 2021. But the number of pipeline candidates does not translate directly into success in developing and commercialising a drug. As of 2022, the only Korean-developed drugs achieving global commercialisation with exports above KRW 100 billion (on a single-product basis) were Celltrion's three biosimilars — Remsima, Herzuma and Truxima — and Daewoong's Nabota, and among traditional Korean pharmaceutical companies Nabota stands alone.

Korean drugs struggle in advanced markets because of low R&D investment and low research efficiency relative to global firms. The top ten global pharma and bio companies averaged R&D at 19.8% of revenue in 2021, far above the 9.65% average of Korea's top ten traditional pharmaceutical companies in 2022. With a revenue structure centred on generics and traded goods in the domestic market, traditional Korean pharmaceutical companies had operating margins of just 6.3%, leaving little room for R&D investment. This study analyses why Nabota succeeded within these constraints and offers implications for Korea's pharmaceutical industry.

2. Theoretical background

2.1 Absorptive capacity

Cohen and Levinthal (1990) defined absorptive capacity as a firm's ability to recognise the value of new external information, assimilate it and apply it to commercial ends, with the organisation's prior related knowledge as its foundation. Zahra and George (2002) recast this from a dynamic capabilities perspective, dividing absorptive capacity into potential absorptive capacity (PACAP: acquisition and assimilation) and realised absorptive capacity (RACAP: transformation and exploitation). Potential absorptive capacity is shaped by the sources of external knowledge, their complementarity and past experience; converting it into realised absorptive capacity requires social integration mechanisms; and activation triggers that bring potential absorptive capacity into play, together with an appropriability regime that protects the results, determine sustainable competitive advantage. This study uses that model as its framework for analysing Nabota.

2.2 Open innovation

Chesbrough's (2006) open innovation is a strategy that uses knowledge flows into and out of a firm to accelerate internal innovation and to expand the markets for external use of innovation. It divides into inbound openness, bringing technology in from outside, and outbound openness, sending internal technology out to be commercialised through another business model (technology sales, spin-offs). It is especially active in the pharmaceutical and bio industry, where new drug R&D costs are rising sharply and research efficiency is falling.

3. Market analysis: the botulinum toxin market

The global aesthetic medicine market is driven by the growth of cosmetic procedures, and the largest and fastest-growing among them is botulinum toxin treatment. Botulinum toxin products account for 43.2% of all cosmetic procedures and grew 34.3% between 2015 and 2020. The global botulinum toxin market was worth about USD 7.2 billion in 2022, having grown at a CAGR of 9.56% over ten years, and is expected to reach USD 18 billion by 2032.

The market is an oligopoly in which Allergan's Botox holds 75–80% of the global share. Botox has secured the most indications in both aesthetic and therapeutic use, and botulinum toxin has a complex protein structure that makes biosimilar development difficult. The pharmaceutical market is also conservative: prescribers do not switch unless there is a marked difference in efficacy or safety, and tend to follow the prescribing of key opinion leaders (KOLs), so years of accumulated clinical data and familiar injection techniques act as a barrier to new entrants. The Korean market, by contrast, is an unusual red ocean where 16 companies and 37 products compete and prices fall — the background to Korean developers' aggressive attempts to expand overseas.

4. Nabota's competitiveness and development history

Nabota is a botulinum toxin product Daewoong developed in-house over five years, approved in Korea in 2013 and granted FDA approval in the United States in 2019. In the US it is sold under the brand Jeuveau through its partner Evolus. Nabota's competitive advantages come down to three points. First, it is the only FDA-approved product with the same 900 kDa molecular weight as Botox, and was the first to be formally verified for 1:1 dosing with Botox, making product switching easy. Second, it demonstrated non-inferiority in a global phase III trial (EV-003) comparing it directly against Botox as the active comparator. Third, it is priced at 75–80% of Botox.

Nabota began as a project in 2008. Building on prior experience in bio drug research, development and production and on knowledge gained from importing and selling Allergan's Botox, the company went from starting development in 2008 to domestic marketing approval in November 2013 in just five years. It then launched a dedicated Nabota business division, moving faster than its peers through cGMP construction and inspection and US marketing approval, and by the end of 2023 had achieved revenue of KRW 142.1 billion, third place by share in the US market and launches in 60 countries worldwide.

5. Daewoong's absorptive capacity

5.1 Sources of external knowledge, complementarity and experience

Daewoong exclusively imported and sold Botox from Allergan for 14 years, from 1995 to 2009, accumulating knowledge of botulinum toxin development, regulatory approval and the market. Through the import approval process it received, reviewed and internalised the same safety and efficacy documentation and production and purification process data as for a new drug, and through its network of Botox-injecting physicians and KOLs it gathered clinical field information on efficacy, side effects and patient satisfaction by product. In addition, through EGF External Solution — Korea's first bio new drug, developed from 1988 and launched in 2001 — it built core bio production process technologies such as culture, purification and analysis ahead of others in Korea, accumulating 30 years of prior knowledge in bio drugs, where production is itself part of the product.

5.2 The activation trigger — Allergan reclaiming distribution rights

In 2008 Allergan announced that it would reclaim the Botox distribution rights, with three years still to run, paying KRW 19 billion in penalties to do so, and sell directly. Losing the rights to Botox — a blockbuster generating KRW 18 billion in revenue as a single product — was a serious crisis for Daewoong and the decisive impetus for deciding to develop its own product. Daewoong put KRW 12 billion of the KRW 19 billion penalty into developing Nabota. As Zahra and George (2002) note, the stronger the activation trigger the more a firm invests in building absorptive capacity, and the loss of Botox and other distribution rights did indeed lead to a sharp rise in Daewoong's R&D investment.

5.3 Absorptive capacity — R&D intensity

On R&D intensity (R&D spending over revenue), the proxy for absorptive capacity, Daewoong jumped from 7.03% in 2010 to 11.72% in 2011 and continued to raise it steadily, reaching 17.34% by the end of 2022 — the highest R&D investment relative to revenue among traditional Korean pharmaceutical companies. It also holds first place in investment per R&D employee, which controls for the effect of workforce size.

5.4 Social integration mechanism — the dedicated Nabota division

Daewoong is the only Korean pharmaceutical company running a project-based research organisation, and its origin is the Nabota programme. The Nabota division permanently assigned research, development (regulatory) and production teams to Nabota alone and tied compensation to the project's success — an autonomous team structure in which a division-head-level project manager holds full control over people, resources, personnel and evaluation with a single, unified decision-making line. Information flowed freely under a shared context and shared prior knowledge, converting potential absorptive capacity into realised absorptive capacity. As a result Nabota obtained US approval one year and nine months after filing (Hanmi's Rolontis took four years). On the strength of this success, Daewoong reorganised its new drug centre into project-based "extreme teams" as well.

5.5 Appropriability regime — patents and lead time

The core of Nabota's manufacture is "Hi-Pure Technology" (registered patent KR1339349), which purifies botulinum toxin to more than 98%; the patent was registered first in Korea and then in the United States, Europe, China, Brazil and elsewhere. In the drying stage after purification, Daewoong uses vacuum drying — adopted only by Allergan and Daewoong — to minimise inactive protein, securing an advantage in efficacy and resistance. Daewoong is also the only one of Korea's 16 botulinum toxin developers to hold both FDA approval and cGMP certification, and this rapid lead time became an appropriability device: while competitors prepared their applications, Daewoong accumulated production know-how and built up KOL-centred treatment cases and clinical data.

6. Daewoong's open innovation

Daewoong is Korea's most active pharmaceutical company in open innovation, having run its "Open Collaboration" programme in earnest since 2013. Nabota had domestic approval but required fresh clinical trials to enter the United States, so its technology transfer is a case of outbound open innovation. What distinguishes Daewoong's approach is that it partnered not with a large firm but with a venture holding expertise in the field. Evolus, founded in 2012, was a small new company — but it was co-founded by the specialist who had handled Botox's FDA approval at Allergan together with American dermatologists and plastic surgeons, and it had an equity base (Strathspey Crown) of some 400 KOLs from the American Society for Aesthetic Plastic Surgery. Daewoong handled product development and production while Evolus handled US and European approvals, marketing and distribution — a complementary structure through which global commercialisation succeeded.

What distinguishes Daewoong's open innovation is that instead of transferring technology to a large company, it partnered with a venture rich in expertise in the field, raising the odds of commercial success in the market — the key success factor and strategy in Nabota's open innovation.

Through this strategy Daewoong generated KRW 109.9 billion in additional revenue from Nabota in 2022, about four times Nabota's domestic revenue that year, while also saving on US clinical development costs. In US dermatology, roughly KRW 78 billion is invested from phase I through approval and phase IV (against KRW 12 billion in development costs to domestic approval for Nabota); Evolus's 10-K filings show about KRW 51.9 billion invested through phase III between 2015 and 2017. Daewoong saved a potential US clinical and regulatory bill of some KRW 78 billion through its partner while maximising Nabota's profit contribution. It has since applied the same strategy to therapeutic toxin (with Aeon Biopharma) and to the gastro-oesophageal reflux drug fexuprazan (with Neurogastrx).

Implications
  • Prior knowledge and R&D investment: The most important elements of absorptive capacity are prior knowledge and the amount of effort. Daewoong had prior knowledge from importing and selling Botox and from developing and producing bio drugs, and it secured absorptive capacity continuously through R&D investment high even among leading pharmaceutical companies, succeeding in developing an excellent product.
  • Organisational structure (social integration mechanism): A cross-functional team able to keep information and knowledge flowing and to concentrate on the project — the dedicated Nabota division — converted potential absorptive capacity into realised absorptive capacity, achieving efficiency in both time and cost in new drug development. This was an unusual attempt requiring a decision from top management.
  • Open innovation grounded in objective judgement: Open innovation that partners with a firm rich in expertise in the field, regardless of its size, on the basis of an accurate assessment of one's own capabilities and the external capabilities needed, raises the odds of global commercialisation.
  • Limitations: Limited access to Daewoong's internal information constrained detailed analysis of which activities within the organisation affected absorptive capacity, and the ongoing strain dispute (the Daewoong–Medytox litigation) was not addressed.
